Formulas & Strategies

A quick reference to use while studying. Note: on the real digital SAT, the Geometry reference formulas (circle, triangle, and box formulas below marked "provided") are given to you on-screen — everything else you should know cold.

Math
Reading & Writing
Algebra
Slope-intercept form
y = mx + b
m = slope, b = y-intercept.
Slope between two points
m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
Point-slope form
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
Standard form of a line
Ax + By = C
Parallel vs. perpendicular slopes
Parallel lines: equal slopes. Perpendicular lines: slopes are negative reciprocals (m₁·m₂ = −1).
System with no solution
Same slope, different y-intercept → lines are parallel, never intersect.
System with infinite solutions
Same slope AND same y-intercept → identical lines.
Advanced Math
Quadratic formula
x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a
For ax² + bx + c = 0.
Discriminant
Δ = b² − 4ac
Δ > 0: two real solutions. Δ = 0: one solution. Δ < 0: no real solutions.
Vertex of a parabola
x = −b / 2a
Plug back in for the y-coordinate (max/min value).
Vertex form
y = a(x − h)² + k
Vertex at (h, k).
Difference of squares
a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)
Exponent rules
xᵃ·xᵇ = x^(a+b)   (xᵃ)ᵇ = x^(ab)   xᵃ/xᵇ = x^(a−b)
Exponential growth/decay
y = a(1 ± r)ᵗ
+r for growth, −r for decay; a = initial value.
Problem-Solving & Data Analysis
Percent change
% change = (new − old) / old × 100
Mean
mean = sum of values / number of values
Median / Mode
Median: middle value when sorted (average of two middle values if even count). Mode: most frequent value.
Probability
P(event) = favorable outcomes / total outcomes
Unit conversion / rates
Set up ratios so units cancel: keep the unit you want to end with in the numerator.
Line of best fit
Slope = rate of change of y per unit x; y-intercept = predicted value when x = 0.
Geometry & Trigonometry
Area of a triangle provided
A = ½ · base · height
Circle: area & circumference provided
A = πr²   C = 2πr
Pythagorean theorem provided
a² + b² = c²
Special right triangles provided
45°-45°-90°: legs : hypotenuse = 1 : 1 : √2.   30°-60°-90°: sides = 1 : √3 : 2.
Volume: box, cylinder, sphere provided
V = lwh   V = πr²h   V = (4/3)πr³
SOH-CAH-TOA
sin = opp/hyp   cos = adj/hyp   tan = opp/adj
Sum of interior angles
(n − 2) × 180°
For an n-sided polygon.
Math Strategies
Plug in the answer choices
For "solve for x" problems, especially with awkward algebra, test choices starting with the middle value (C/B) and adjust up/down.
Plug in your own numbers
For problems with variables in the answer choices, pick simple numbers (avoid 0, 1, and numbers already in the problem), solve numerically, then match.
Use the calculator strategically
It's available on both modules now, but mental math and estimation are often faster for simple arithmetic — save the calculator for actual computation.
Read the question twice
Watch for what's actually being asked (e.g. "what is the value of x + 3", not just x) — a common trap.
Skip and return
Flag time-consuming questions and come back after finishing easier ones — every question is worth the same amount.
Information and Ideas
Central ideas & details
Find the main claim first (often in the first or last sentence), then check that your answer choice matches the passage's overall point — not just a detail from it.
Command of evidence
The correct answer must directly and specifically support the claim in question — eliminate choices that are true but irrelevant to the specific claim.
Inferences
The right inference is a small, logical step from the text — not a leap. If it requires outside knowledge or a big assumption, it's wrong.
Craft and Structure
Words in context
Cover the answer choices, predict your own word from context first, then find the closest match — this avoids being tricked by a "common" definition that doesn't fit here.
Text structure & purpose
Ask "why did the author include this sentence/paragraph?" rather than "what does it say?"
Cross-text connections
Identify each text's stance/claim separately before comparing — most questions hinge on where the two texts agree or disagree.
Expression of Ideas
Rhetorical synthesis
Reread the stated goal carefully — most wrong choices are true statements from the notes that just don't accomplish the specific goal asked for.
Transitions
Determine the logical relationship between the two ideas first (contrast, cause/effect, addition, example) before looking at the choices.
Standard English Conventions
Subject-verb agreement
Find the true subject — ignore prepositional phrases and interrupters between subject and verb.
Comma rules
Nonessential clauses/appositives need commas on both sides. Never separate a subject from its verb with a single comma.
Semicolons
Use to join two independent clauses with no conjunction, or a FANBOYS conjunction, not with a comma alone (comma splice).
Pronoun agreement
Match the pronoun's number (singular/plural) to its antecedent, and make sure the antecedent itself is unambiguous.
Apostrophes
"its" = possessive, "it's" = it is. Plural noun ('dogs') vs. possessive ('dog's') vs. plural possessive ('dogs'').
Reading & Writing Strategies
Answer the question, not the passage
The correct answer must directly answer what's asked — not just be a true statement about the passage.
Eliminate aggressively
Look for extreme language ("always," "never," "proves") and answers that are half-right/half-wrong — both are common wrong-choice patterns.
Manage your pace
Each Reading & Writing question is a short, self-contained passage — budget roughly 1 minute per question and flag anything you're stuck on.
